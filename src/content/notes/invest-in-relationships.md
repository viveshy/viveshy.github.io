One thing I have learned from my brother is the importance of investing
in relationships. In both personal and professional life, you need people
who encourage you, support you, and stand by you. In this note, I want to
keep the focus on professional relationships.

When I say you have to invest in relationships, I do not mean you should
fake it. I do not mean you should go after every person in the
organization and forcefully build relationships. No. It's not like that.

You should have a good relationship with your manager, your lead, your
juniors, and your teammates. Be someone people can talk to, trust, and
rely on. Over time, if you do this genuinely, you will probably earn two
or three genuine friends along the way. And that matters.

You cannot do everything alone. There will be times when you are behind
a deadline and need someone to help you. There will be times when you are
given a critical task and need people to support you, sometimes by
pushing themselves a little harder simply because they care about you and
the outcome.

Not because they are obligated to. Not because you are senior. Not
because they are afraid of you. But because there is mutual respect and
genuine goodwill. That is what investing in relationships means to me.

Otherwise, you end up building purely transactional relationships where
everything is taken literally: "That's not my responsibility." "You
didn't ask me." "It's not in my scope." "I finished my part." And while
those things may technically be correct, the industry does not work
purely on technicalities.

Work is done by people. Sometimes you need someone to go a little beyond
their responsibility. Sometimes you need someone to understand what you
mean without explaining every little thing. Sometimes you need someone to
say, "Don't worry, I've got you."

You cannot demand that kind of support. You earn it over time.

So invest in people. Not strategically. Not because you might need them
someday. Do it genuinely.

Because when you build good relationships, you are not just building a
network. You are building a support system.
